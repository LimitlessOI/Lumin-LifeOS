## 1. Pattern after the disclosure lag (KNOW)

| Finding | Source class | Implication for copy-trades |
|---------|--------------|-----------------------------|
| **Aggregate Congress does not beat the market** post–STOCK Act | Belmont et al. (J. Public Econ. 2022): House buys −26 bps / 6mo; sells −11 bps; accused senators no edge. NBER w35041 (trades 2012–2023): little meaningful excess | Blind “copy everyone” loses or flat vs SPY |
| **Pre–STOCK Act** (pre-2012) some studies found big abnormal returns for powerful members | Karadas (2019), Huang/Xu | Edge largely gone after disclosure law |
| **Measured from disclosure date**, buying *all* purchases: **~−2.4% alpha vs SPY at 180 days** (win rate ~43%) | Signal Congress, n≈10.9k purchases 2018–2026 | Exact mirror of “everyone” underperforms |
| **Filtered signals** (cross-chamber bipartisan clusters): **~+6.2% alpha at 180 days** (win ~51%, n≈622) | Same study | Filters matter more than “Congress” brand |
| **Fast disclosure ≤7 days:** only **~+1% alpha** at 180d | Same | Slightly better, not a goldmine |
| **Disclosure itself** can move prices ~17–29 bps (esp. committee-relevant buys) | Xu et al. (legislative/fiscal expectations paper) | Part of the move is already priced when you see the filing |
| **Politicians’ own returns ≠ your copy returns** | TrueWallet-style delay analyses | Their fill price can be far better; you buy after the move |

**Bottom line (KNOW):** After up to 45 days, the *average* disclosed buy is **not** a free lunch. Edge, if any, is in **filters** (leadership, bipartisan clusters, large size) and in **concentrated tech bets** that some famous names held — not in raw “do what they did.”

## 2. Can we know sooner? (honest)

| Idea | Reality |
|------|---------|
| See their trade the day they trade | **No** (legal). STOCK Act is the public pipe; private early access = insider trading |
| Shorter than 45 days | Many file earlier; median lag often ~2–4 weeks, not always 45. Still after the fact |
| Proxy “sooner” signals (legal) | Committee agenda / bill text, USASpending / contracts, lobbying, 13F / insider Form 4, unusual options flow — **correlated guesses**, not Congress fills |
| Leadership-weighted post-disclosure drift | Contested; some work (Wei & Zhou / PPTAD research) argues chairs/leaders > rank-and-file; Belmont says aggregate still weak |

**KNOW:** You cannot legally get Congress fills before disclosure.  
**THINK:** Best “earlier” edge is **non-Congress** public data + **act the day the PTR appears** (not wait another week).

## 5. Direct answer to Adam

1. **After 45 days:** average disclosed purchase **underperforms** SPY; filtered clusters can show **~+6 pts / 6 months** alpha; famous names’ **copy** returns are often still strong in **tech bulls** but that is mostly **sector bet + lag**, not proven perpetual insider edge.  
2. **Know sooner?** Only via **other public proxies** or **faster filings** — never their private ticket.  
3. **100 accounts following “exactly what they do” for a year:**  
   - Blind everyone → **worse than SPY** (THINK).  
   - Top politicians equity-only → **~market to modest beat** in recent years (THINK), with high concentration risk.  
   - Options-exact → **not replicable** at $300–$2k and results would be a **dispersion cloud**, not one CAGR.  
4. **Would we have gotten rich?** Unlikely vs simply owning QQQ/SPY in the same AI years. **Would we have learned?** Yes — especially if we A/B: blind vs bipartisan filter vs NANC benchmark on the same 100 accounts.

## 7. “They have insider info” — channel vs measured trading profit

Adam (2026-07-27): it’s an absolute fact they sit on committees and know where contracts go.

| Layer | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| **Access / information** | **KNOW — real.** Committee members see bills, markups, classified briefings, procurement direction, regulatory calendars before the public. Academic work links politician ownership to contracts/grants/trade-mission selection (pre–STOCK Act especially). Disclosure of committee-relevant buys can move prices (fiscal-expectations channel). |
| **Using that for personal trading** | **Illegal under STOCK Act** in principle; **enforcement is weak** (small fines, late filings common) — so behavior can still happen. |
| **Showing up as beat-the-market trading after 2012** | **Not an absolute fact in the aggregates.** Best large-sample studies find little/no superior returns for Congress overall post–STOCK Act. Pre-2012 evidence of abnormal returns is stronger. |
| **Why both can be true** | Knowing ≠ always trading on it; trading on it ≠ always showing in disclosed equity (options, trusts, timing, noise); edge may be in **favors to firms** (contracts) more than in **your ability to copy late disclosures**; mega-cap tech concentration can look like “skill” without needing a classified tip. |

## 8. Disclosure-bump scalp (Adam 2026-07-27)

**Thesis:** Even if alpha is only tens of bps, buy **immediately** on PTR/disclosure (or award headline), hold through the **typical short bump**, sell. Small $ × many events × many accounts = money if history is measured well.

| Piece | Plan |
|-------|------|
| Entry | Disclosure hit (Quiver/OSS webhook or poll ≤1–5 min) → marketable limit on **liquid** names only |
| Exit | Time/stop based on **measured** median bump window (e.g. minutes–few days — calibrate from history, don’t guess) |
| Sizing | Can fan across many stock-lane accounts **only** if liquidity + broker allows; prefer one logical book sliced into accounts for P&L attribution |
| Gate | Skip if expected bump &lt; 2× round-trip cost (spread + fees + slippage) |

### Honest math (THINK)

| Item | Ballpark |
|------|----------|
| Academic disclosure bump | ~**17–29 bps** (0.17–0.29%) on average purchase disclosures |
| Round-trip cost on small tickets | Often **5–20+ bps** (worse on illiquid names) |
| Edge after costs | **Thin or zero** unless: liquid mega-caps, tight spreads, high hit-rate subset (committee / defense / clustered) |
| On ~$115k fleet @ +20 bps **gross** | ~**$230** per clean event before costs — real, not life-changing; stacks with frequency |

**KNOW:** The bump exists in studies.  
**THINK:** Blind “all accounts, every disclosure” will often **give the edge back to the market maker**.  
**Test design:** Backtest + paper: median return from disclosure_t0 → t+1h / t+1d / t+3d by liquidity bucket; only trade when historical expectancy &gt; cost × safety factor.
