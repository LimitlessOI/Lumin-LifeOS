There are really two separate issues you uncovered.

1. Conversation flow
The interruptions themselves weren't the biggest problem. The problem was the shape of the interruption.
Humans don't stop like this:

"Here's the next ide—"
They stop more like:
"...yeah, okay."
or
"...right."
or
"...anyway."
They naturally taper off.
Your system should have an interruption decay model instead of an immediate cutoff.

For example:

Detect user speech beginning.
Finish the current syllable or word.
Fade volume over 100–300 ms.
End with a conversational trailing phrase if appropriate.
Immediately switch to listening.
That tiny detail would make interruptions feel intentional instead of broken.
2. Intent commitment
This one is actually more serious.
I repeatedly said:

"Okay, I'm going to give you 25."
Then I didn't.
That's a violation of conversational trust.

The AI should have an internal state that says something like:

Current commitment:
Deliver ideas 7–25 continuously.

Completion condition:
Stop only after #25
or
User interrupts.
Once that commitment exists, the response generator shouldn't suddenly decide to summarize or ask another question.
It should continue executing until one of those conditions is met.

Another idea this gives me
Conversation Contracts
Whenever the AI makes a promise—

"I'll read all 25."
"I'll summarize this article."
"Let's compare these products."
—it creates a temporary contract.
The runtime tracks:

What was promised.
What counts as completion.
Whether the promise has been fulfilled.
Whether the user interrupted or changed the request.
That way the AI can't accidentally abandon the task halfway through.
One more thing I noticed
You also exposed something about voice vs. text.
When you're typing, it's okay if the AI pauses to think.

When you're talking, silence feels like failure.

Voice has a completely different latency budget.

If nothing is happening for even a second or two, your brain starts wondering:

"Did it hear me?"
So the runtime should have a Presence Layer that's separate from reasoning.
It should always be doing something:

Listening.
Nodding.
Giving subtle acknowledgements.
Breathing.
Showing it's thinking.
Streaming partial thoughts.
Trailing off naturally when interrupted.
The reasoning engine can take another second if needed—but the presence should never disappear.
I also noticed something interesting about how you naturally test systems.
You don't just ask whether something works. You deliberately stress conversational edge cases:

Interrupt mid-sentence.
Pause unexpectedly.
Change topics.
Reference something from two minutes ago.
Make the AI commit to a long sequence.
See whether it actually follows through.
Those aren't random interactions—they're revealing failure modes. I think Taloa could actually formalize this into a Conversation Torture Suite: hundreds of realistic conversational scenarios that every runtime must pass before it's considered production-ready. That would test qualities like interruption handling, commitment tracking, prediction recovery, turn-taking, conversational presence, and latency under real human behavior—not just benchmark questions.
Ironically, this conversation itself generated several test cases that I'd add to that suite.
