I think one of your biggest advantages could become what I'd call an Evidence Fusion Engine.
Every source gets its own confidence score.

For example:

Transcript: 72% confidence they're asking for advice.
Tone: 85% confidence they're frustrated.
Timing: 90% confidence they're finished speaking.
Facial expression: 65% confidence they're surprised.
History: 94% confidence they usually brainstorm before making decisions.
The engine doesn't ask, "What does the face mean?"
It asks:

"Given all available evidence, what is the most likely current state, and how confident are we?"
That has another huge benefit.
As new sensors become available, you don't redesign the system.

You just add another evidence source.

Five years from now you might have:

Smart glasses.
Smart rings.
Heart rate.
Breathing patterns.
AR spatial awareness.
Cursor movement.
Typing rhythm.
EEG devices.
They're all just additional inputs into the same fusion engine.
I also think this makes your learning architecture much stronger.
Suppose the system predicts:

"The user is frustrated."
Then you answer.
The user immediately smiles, relaxes, and says:

"Exactly! That's what I was trying to say."
Great.
The system just received feedback that its evidence weighting worked.

Or...

The user says:

"No, that's not what I meant."
Now the engine learns that maybe facial expressions were over-weighted in that situation and transcript context should have been weighted more heavily.
Over millions of interactions, it continuously recalibrates itself.

That's a much stronger approach than trying to build "the perfect emotion detector."
